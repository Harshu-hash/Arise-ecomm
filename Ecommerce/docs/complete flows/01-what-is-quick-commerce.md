# What Is Quick Commerce?

## What Is This?

Quick commerce means ordering everyday items — groceries, snacks, household essentials, personal care products — and getting them delivered to your home **very fast**, usually within minutes. Instead of planning a big weekly shop, customers order what they need right now, and a nearby shop prepares the order while a delivery partner brings it to their door.

This platform is a complete system that makes that possible. It connects customers, local shops, delivery riders, and business operators in one place.

---

## How Is Quick Commerce Different from Regular Online Shopping?

| Regular e-commerce        | Quick commerce                              |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Delivery in days          | Delivery in minutes to an hour              |
| Large warehouses far away | Nearby local shops or dark stores           |
| Big planned orders        | Small, urgent, everyday orders              |
| Standard shipping         | Dedicated delivery partners on two-wheelers |
| Wide product range        | Focus on essentials and daily needs         |

The whole system is built around **speed** and **proximity**. Products come from shops near the customer, and delivery partners are always moving around the city ready to pick up orders.

## Who Is Involved?

Four groups of people make the platform work:

### 1. Customers

People who want to buy things quickly. They use the customer app to browse products, place orders, pay, and track delivery.

### 2. Sellers (Shops)

Local stores or partner shops that list their products on the platform. When a customer orders, the seller prepares and packs the items.

### 3. Delivery Partners (Riders)

People on bikes or scooters who pick up packed orders from shops and deliver them to customers. They earn money per delivery.

### 4. Platform Admin

The team that runs the business — approving new sellers and riders, managing the catalog, handling money, resolving problems, and configuring how the platform works.

## How Does the Platform Make Money?

The platform typically earns revenue through:

- **Delivery fees** — charged to the customer based on distance
- **Platform fees** — a small service charge on each order
- **Commission** — a percentage taken from what the seller earns
- **Other charges** — such as packaging or convenience fees, depending on configuration

Sellers earn from product sales (minus platform commission). Delivery partners earn per trip plus any tips customers add.

## A Typical Order Journey (In One Minute)

Here is what happens when a customer orders a few items:

1. **Customer** opens the app, browses products from a nearby shop, and adds items to the cart.
2. **Customer** goes to checkout, chooses a delivery address, applies a coupon if they have one, and pays online or chooses cash on delivery.
3. **System** creates the order and notifies the **seller**.
4. **Seller** accepts the order within about a minute and starts packing.
5. **System** finds a nearby **delivery partner** who is online and available.
6. **Rider** accepts the job, goes to the shop, picks up the packed order.
7. **Rider** navigates to the customer's address and delivers the order.
8. **Customer** shares a delivery code to confirm they received the order.
9. **System** settles money — seller gets their earnings (held briefly for returns), rider gets delivery fee, platform keeps its share.

The entire journey can take as little as 15–30 minutes in a well-run operation.

## What Makes This Platform Complete?

Unlike a simple online store, this platform includes everything needed to run a real quick commerce business:

- **Four separate apps** for customers, sellers, riders, and admins
- **Real-time order updates** so everyone sees changes instantly
- **Live delivery tracking** on a map
- **Online payments** and **cash on delivery**
- **Digital wallets** for customers, sellers, and riders
- **Automatic timers** — if a seller does not accept in time, the order cancels automatically
- **Return and refund** handling
- **Support chat** between customers and the help team
- **Marketing tools** — coupons, offers, banners, curated home page sections
- **Fleet management** — admins can see all active riders on a map

## Why Would Someone Build This?

If you want to start a business like Blinkit, Zepto, Swiggy Instamart, or any hyperlocal delivery service, you need more than a shopping website. You need:

- A way for shops to manage inventory and orders
- A way for riders to get jobs and navigate
- A way to handle money across all parties
- A way to track orders in real time
- A way for operators to manage everything

This documentation describes exactly how such a system works — feature by feature, flow by flow — so you can understand it fully and recreate something similar.
